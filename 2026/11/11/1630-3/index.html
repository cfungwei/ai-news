Paper proposes world in-context learning for robot manipulation

researchrobotics (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

A new paper introduces "world in-context learning," letting robots use in-context demonstrations without being tied to the exact scene they were recorded in, aiming to match the performance of trained manipulation policies.

Trained action policies encode manipulation skills into network weights, tying what they learn to the distribution they were trained on. In-context learning (ICL) avoids that coupling but has lagged behind trained policies because a demonstration recorded in one scene offers no feedback from the world the robot currently faces. The authors observe that what an agent learns in context is largely semantic -- task steps and object roles -- rather than tied to visual appearance, meaning experience from one scene can transfer to another.
