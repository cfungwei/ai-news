STEPQuant paper tackles quantization errors in linear-attention recurrent states

researchmodels (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

A new paper introduces STEPQuant, studying how quantization errors in delta-rule linear-attention models' recurrent states vary by when and where they occur, aiming to cut memory use without hurting accuracy.

More

Linear attention models replace growing KV caches with fixed-size recurrent states, but these states become a memory bottleneck during concurrent serving. Directly quantizing them to low precision has previously caused accuracy to degrade because errors compound across decoding steps. The authors find that error impact depends on two dimensions: how long an error persists in memory, and the state's structural position. STEPQuant proposes a quantization approach informed by these findings. Benchmark results are not disclosed in the available excerpt.

GRACE paper proposes generation-aware latent compression for video diffusion

A new paper, GRACE, targets the trade-off between compression and quality in video autoencoders used by diffusion transformers, aiming to speed up video generation without costly retraining.

More

Highly compressed video autoencoders can speed up video diffusion models since the diffusion transformer processes fewer tokens, but higher compression typically hurts reconstruction quality and needs more channels, slowing convergence. Because a new compressed latent differs from what a pretrained diffusion transformer was trained on, that model usually must be retrained or adapted. GRACE is framed as a generation-aware approach to this compression problem. The excerpt does not specify exact compression ratios or quality results achieved.
