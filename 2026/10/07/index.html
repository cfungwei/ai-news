Study finds self-generated feedback destabilizes test-time training

researchmodels (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

Researchers show that letting language models learn from their own outputs during test-time training worsens predictions on independent text, across model sizes from 125M to Qwen3-4B.

More

Test-time training lets a model update its own weights during inference, but the paper finds a causal problem: when the model learns from its own generated output, each update also changes the model that produces the next training example. Across 128K-token streams, retaining these self-generated updates worsened prediction on independent, human-written text in three TTT end-to-end configurations (125M, 760M, and 3B parameters). The same failure appeared when the Adam optimizer updated Qwen3-4B's existing weights, suggesting the issue isn't specific to one architecture or optimizer. The paper frames this as a causal decomposition of long-horizon adaptation, implying it identifies which part of the training loop causes the instability.

The method targets diffusion language models (DLMs) by minimizing Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) between the distribution of generated text and reference text, measured in the feature space of a frozen pretrained DLM. To estimate MMD, the approach retains contextual features at individual token positions, yielding multiple observations per sequence from a single pass through the feature extractor.
