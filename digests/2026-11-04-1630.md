### Paper proposes 'Route Anything' for routing across AI models and policies
`research` `agents` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

A new paper studies how AI systems can dynamically select and coordinate specialized models, tools, and policies rather than relying on one model for every task. It frames this routing problem as key to building general-purpose AI systems.

<details><summary>More</summary>

The paper, called 'Route Anything,' argues that a promising path to general-purpose AI is orchestrating a heterogeneous mix of specialized models, tools, and policies rather than relying on one model to do everything. The study treats this as a unified routing problem spanning both model and policy selection within one AI system.

### Study introduces Pandora, an LLM simulator for less self-aware therapy clients
`research` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

Researchers built Pandora, a framework for simulating psychotherapy clients whose self-understanding is incomplete, addressing a flaw in current LLM-based client simulators that over-explain distress clearly. It aims to better reflect how real clients express problems before they can articulate them.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Current large language model-based client simulators tend to rely on clinically organized case descriptions, which can push the simulated client to explain their difficulties more clearly than they would actually understand them in the moment. The researchers note that real psychotherapy clients often feel distress before they can coherently describe it, and that how they express it shifts with emotion, perceived safety, and trust. Pandora is proposed as a framework that models how psychological experience becomes expressed under these more realistic, 'self-opaque' conditions.

- [Hugging Face Daily Papers: Words Caught in the Heart: Simulating Person-Specific Expression for Self-Opaque Clients in Psychotherapy](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.00617)

</details>

### DreamX repurposes video diffusion models to learn universal robot actions
`research` `robotics` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

A new paper introduces DreamX, which repurposes a pretrained video diffusion model to learn action representations from video that transfer across different robot embodiments and appearances. It aims to fix existing latent action models that entangle actions with irrelevant visual details.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Learning a universal action representation from video requires capturing dynamics that stay consistent across different executions of the same action while ignoring changes in embodiment and appearance. The paper argues existing latent action models fall short because they infer actions from visual transitions in a way that entangles the true action with irrelevant visual variation, limiting how well they transfer to new embodiments. DreamX instead repurposes a pretrained video diffusion model as what the authors call a universal latent action learner.

- [Hugging Face Daily Papers: DreamX: Video Diffusion Model is a Universal Latent Action Learner](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.05377)

</details>

### Axion 1.0 debuts as a fully open agent foundation model with a simplified training recipe
`models` `agents` `open-source` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

Axion 1.0 is a new fully open agent foundation model that reaches strong, broad agentic capability using just three training stages: two supervised fine-tuning stages and reinforcement learning. It skips mid-training, DPO, and on-policy distillation used by comparable models.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Axion 1.0 is presented as a fully open agent foundation model, with its creators emphasizing a deliberately simple training recipe rather than a more elaborate pipeline. The model is trained in just three stages: two lightweight supervised fine-tuning stages followed by reinforcement learning, skipping dedicated mid-training, direct preference optimization, and on-policy distillation. The first fine-tuning stage is designed to build strong, self-contained reasoning through model-aware data construction. The paper positions this as a simplified alternative to the more complex recipes typically used to train agentic models.

- [Hugging Face Daily Papers: Axion 1.0: Open Agent Foundation Models, Simplified](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03233)

## 🦾 Robotics

### RoboColosseum benchmarks 5 robot policies and 3 AI agents across real robots
`robotics` `research` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

RoboColosseum is a new distributed evaluation framework that tests general-purpose robot policies across a network of real, locally operated robots using standardized protocols. Its initial study evaluates five vision-language-action policies and three frontier AI agents across four robot embodiments.

<details><summary>More</summary>

The paper notes that general-purpose robot policies need to work across different robots, tasks, and environments, but the real-world variation that makes physical trials meaningful also makes them hard to reproduce. RoboColosseum addresses this with a distributed evaluation framework combining unified policy serving and standardized testing protocols across a network of locally operated robots in diverse settings. Its initial study evaluates five vision-language-action (VLA) policies alongside three frontier AI agents across four different robot embodiments.

- [Hugging Face Daily Papers: RoboColosseum: Evaluating Physical Intelligence at Scale In-The-Wild](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.02497)

</details>
