## 📄 Research

### Claude solves 25-year-old mystery behind NSA's digital-signature seeds
`research` `models` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

Anthropic says Claude figured out that the unexplained seed values behind NIST's standard elliptic curves came from a basic C random number generator, easing backdoor suspicions.

<details><summary>More</summary>

The seeds behind NIST's standard curves for digital signatures were supplied by the NSA decades ago without explanation, fueling long-standing suspicion that they could hide a deliberate backdoor. According to Anthropic, Claude determined in under 30 minutes that the values originated from "lrand48," a simple, well-known C random number generator. That finding suggests the seeds were generated through an ordinary, non-secret process rather than one designed to embed a weakness. It doesn't fully rule out a backdoor, but it removes one of the main pieces of circumstantial evidence cited over the past 25 years.

- [The Decoder: Anthropics' Claude cracks a 25-year-old NSA cryptography mystery in under 30 minutes](https://the-decoder.com/anthropics-claude-cracks-a-25-year-old-nsa-cryptography-mystery-in-under-30-minutes/)
