### vLLM Semantic Router team releases Decision 3.0, open decision models 0.6B-27B
`models` `open-source` `tools` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

The vLLM Semantic Router team released Decision 3.0 (d3), Apache-2.0 multimodal decision models from 0.6B to 27B parameters that read text, images, and video.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Decision 3.0 is built on Qwen3.5 (0.8B, 2B, 4B, and 9B) and Qwen3.8-27B, with vision encoders adding 0.33B to the 4B model and 0.46B to the 27B model. The models support three decision types: choice, yes/no, and score, returning a probability for every answer without generating text. They can answer several questions about the same input in a single call. The team says the family ranks #1 on both Jev Decision Index boards, and the models are released under an Apache-2.0 license.

### DiffusionART paper unifies diffusion RL fine-tuning for efficiency and stability
`research` `models` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

A new paper, DiffusionART, identifies a hidden forward-reverse consistency force behind the tradeoff between efficient and stable RL fine-tuning of diffusion models, and proposes a fix.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Online RL fine-tuning of diffusion models with black-box rewards has split into two families: reverse-process methods like Flow-GRPO, which are stable but inefficient due to poor credit assignment, and forward-process methods like DiffusionNFT, which are efficient but unstable without carefully tuned off-policy training. The paper's authors trace this tradeoff to a hidden forward-reverse consistency force that joint positive-negative training only partially cancels out. DiffusionART is presented as a method that resolves this to deliver both efficiency and stability, though the available excerpt does not specify the technique's mechanics or benchmark results.

- [Hugging Face Daily Papers: DiffusionART: Efficient, Stable and Scalable Reinforcement of Diffusion Models](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12572)

</details>

### S-Space: researchers find a spatial reasoning subspace inside multimodal models
`research` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

A paper uncovers S-Space, a linear subspace in multimodal language models where object-token representations encode continuous horizontal, vertical, and distance coordinates.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Multimodal language models show promising spatial reasoning, but the internal mechanisms behind it have been poorly understood. The researchers identified S-Space by differentiating spatial-answer scores with respect to object-token activations, revealing axes that track position along three dimensions. They report that S-Space appears to be intrinsic to these models rather than something induced by specific training data or prompting. What remains unclear from the excerpt is how S-Space was validated or whether it generalizes across model families.

- [Hugging Face Daily Papers: S-Space: Exploring Spatial Workspace in Multimodal Language Models](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.13527)

</details>

`robotics` `research` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

A new paper, Reflex, tackles visually grounded reactive control for humanoid robots, aiming to let them anticipate and respond to moving objects within about a second.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Humans routinely react to fast-moving events, such as returning a tennis shot or catching a falling object, by anticipating a target and reflexively responding with their whole body almost instantly. The paper frames this as still difficult for humanoid robots, which must coordinate whole-body motion, react to object dynamics in real time, and ground their perception in an egocentric view. Reflex is proposed as an approach to close that gap, though the excerpt cuts off before describing its method or results in detail.
