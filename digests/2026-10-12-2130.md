## 📄 Research

### Microsoft research: clearing chat history helps AI agents escape dead ends
`research` `agents` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

Microsoft researchers found that AI agents get stuck in "idea basins" as their chat context grows, and that clearing history while keeping workspace state helps them escape. Their Fork-and-Flush method improved results by up to 66 percent on the same compute budget.

<details><summary>More</summary>

The researchers describe "idea basins" as local solution regions that autonomous agents become trapped in as their conversational context accumulates. Their Fork-and-Flush method periodically clears the chat history but preserves the underlying workspace, letting the agent retain progress without carrying forward unproductive context. In tests, this produced up to 66 percent better results for the same compute budget compared to keeping full history. The research did not specify which models or benchmark tasks were used.

## 🤖 Agents

### Qwen releases QwenGyre, an RL framework for long-horizon agent training
`agents` `open-source` `tools` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

Qwen released QwenGyre, an open reinforcement learning framework for training AI agents on extremely long, complex tasks. It pairs an elastic GPU scheduler with a trajectory processor to speed up training without losing partial progress.

<details><summary>More</summary>

QwenGyre targets what it calls "XLong" agent training, tasks that unfold over very long horizons. Its Elastic Scheduler reallocates GPUs between rollout and training as demand shifts, reportedly delivering up to 1.85x speedup over colocated setups and 1.78x over asynchronous ones. Its Trajectory Processor converts branching executions, such as compaction, sub-agents, and retries, into prefix-shared trajectory trees with bounded, deduplicated training samples. Partial progress is preserved even if a task times out.
