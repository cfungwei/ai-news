### SteerDistill quantizes LLM agents using closed-loop teacher steering
`agents` `research` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

A new method called SteerDistill corrects behavioral errors in low-bit quantized LLM agents by using a closed-loop teacher model to steer training, aiming to preserve task success that quantization usually degrades.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Quantizing LLM agents to low-bit precision cuts inference cost but often hurts task success. The paper's analysis of failed rollouts found that quantized agents tend to overlook task requirements or environmental conditions, causing their behavior to diverge from a full-precision reference. Fixing this is hard: offline training data may not cover the student model's specific failure cases, and the student's own rollouts rarely contain enough correct examples in those same contexts. SteerDistill addresses this with a closed-loop process where a teacher model steers the student during training, though the excerpt doesn't detail how much task performance it recovers.

- [Hugging Face Daily Papers: SteerDistill: Quantizing LLM Agents via Closed-Loop Teacher Steering](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.13410)

It tracks evolving information needs and source coverage through what the authors call a Read Intent Protocol, and uses "Adaptive Reader Backends" to decide how evidence is surfaced. <details><summary>More</summary>

Most math training data for language models strips out the structure of the original textbooks, reducing proofs to isolated problem-solution pairs. The resulting corpus holds roughly 380,000 structured proof-supervision records spanning core math subjects. The paper doesn't specify which models were trained or evaluated on the corpus, or how much it improves performance over flattened datasets.
