### MMFlash speeds up speculative decoding for vision-language models
`research` `models` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

A new method, MMFlash, extends speculative decoding to multimodal models by measuring how much each token depends on visual input, addressing a gap left by text-only drafters like DFlash.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Speculative decoding normally uses a lightweight drafter to propose tokens that a larger target model verifies in parallel, but prior block-proposing drafters such as DFlash focused on text only. The authors introduce a metric of token-level visual dependence, based on the KL divergence between next-token predictions with and without visual input. They find that acceptance of drafted tokens varies with how visually dependent a token is, motivating a multimodal-aware approach. The excerpt does not give MMFlash's measured speedup over text-only baselines.

### Paper analyzes how Looped Transformers reuse computation via residuals
`research` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

A new paper examines Looped Transformers, which cut parameter count by reusing shared blocks, and argues that residual connections determine how repeated computation's results get reused.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Looped Transformers save parameters by applying the same blocks repeatedly, but the authors note that parameter sharing alone doesn't decide how later steps reuse earlier results. Taking a 'depth-memory' view of residual connections, they analyze which representation is stored after each block and how later reads weight it. They point out that a full block's output mixes its input with its new update, so reweighting the output also reweights history already carried in the input. The excerpt does not detail the paper's proposed fix or its experimental results.

- [Hugging Face Daily Papers: Loopback Transformer: Towards a Unified View of Residual Looped Transformers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.07226)

</details>

### New benchmark tests text-to-SQL systems' ability to reuse past queries
`research` `tools` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

A new benchmark, HARE, measures whether text-to-SQL systems can adapt earlier queries over the same database to answer a new, related question, rather than treating each question in isolation.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Most text-to-SQL benchmarks evaluate each natural-language question independently, ignoring cases where an earlier query over the same database could supply useful joins or calculations for a later one. The paper introduces HARE (History-AwaRe Text-to-SQL BEnchmark) to measure both the accuracy and the cost of adapting historical SQL to new questions. HARE comprises 1,600 requests built from a fixed pool of 823 verified queries. The excerpt does not report how existing systems perform on the benchmark.

### JVT simplifies pixel-space video diffusion with a plain transformer
`research` `models` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

A new model, JVT (Just Video Transformer), generates video directly in pixel space using a large-patch DiT-style transformer, skipping the pretrained autoencoders and specialized architectures earlier methods relied on.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Direct diffusion modeling of raw, high-dimensional video has typically required specialized architectures such as pretrained autoencoders, auxiliary detail networks, cascaded stages or U-Net-style skip connections. JVT instead uses a simple large-patch, DiT-style transformer with none of these additions. The authors systematically study video patch embeddings, register tokens and adaptive layer normalization to settle on its design. The excerpt does not include JVT's generation quality results compared to existing video diffusion methods.

- [Hugging Face Daily Papers: JVT: Just Video Transformer makes pixel-space video diffusion simple](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.18549)
