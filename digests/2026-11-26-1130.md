### PyRUA-Dex lets coding agents learn dexterous manipulation without demos
`research` `robotics` `agents` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

Researchers present PyRUA-Dex, a framework where coding agents write programmatic policies for dexterous bimanual manipulation, avoiding costly human demonstration data.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Progress on dexterous manipulation has been limited by scarce high-quality demonstrations: retargeting from human video is imprecise, teleoperation gives no force feedback, and exoskeleton-based collection doesn't scale. PyRUA-Dex sidesteps this by having coding agents develop a programmatic policy for the task without any demonstrations. The policy is built in two stages, the first being an exploration phase in which multiple agents explore the task.

- [Hugging Face Daily Papers: PyRUA-Dex: Learning Programmatic Policies for Dexterous Bimanual Manipulation with Coding Agents](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.33144)

</details>

### VibeVideoAgent closes the loop between code-driven video generation and perception
`research` `agents` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

A new method, VibeVideoAgent, feeds rendered video frames and audio back into a coding agent so it can catch rendering errors that don't trigger runtime exceptions.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Code-driven video generation has a coding agent write and run rendering programs, giving precise control over typography, data visualizations, synchronized audio, and multi-shot scenes while leaving an editable project behind. A program that runs without error can still produce overlapping labels, a misaimed camera, broken object interactions, or narration that drifts out of sync, none of which raise a runtime error. VibeVideoAgent addresses this by feeding the rendered frames and audio back into the coding agent, closing what the authors call the perceptual loop.

- [Hugging Face Daily Papers: VibeVideoAgent: Closing the Perceptual Loop for Code-Driven Video Generation](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34237)

</details>

### ICLWorld benchmarks in-context learning for agents across digital and physical tasks
`research` `agents` `robotics` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

ICLWorld is a new online benchmark testing how well AI agents use in-context learning to adapt in real time, spanning both computer-use and robotic tasks.

<details><summary>More</summary>

In-context learning lets agents pick up new rules from context and adapt to user-specific requirements at inference time, and the same frontier agents now operate both computers and robots. The authors argue this ability is still poorly assessed: existing benchmarks are largely offline, rely on text or static images, and risk training-data contamination, while robot evaluations are limited to method-level ablations. ICLWorld is introduced as an online benchmark that evaluates agents' in-context learning from digital to physical settings.

### AIRIS benchmark tests embodied agents' social judgment in streaming video
`agents` `robotics` `research` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

AIRIS is a new causal, streaming benchmark that evaluates whether embodied AI agents can read social cues and decide when, whether, and how to respond around people.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Embodied agents operating around people must recognize social cues and also judge when evidence is sufficient, whether to intervene, and how to respond without breaking social norms. Existing benchmarks typically test these abilities in isolation or only on completed videos, which obscures exactly where an online social decision goes wrong. AIRIS instead frames social embodied intelligence as a time-anchored, causal chain of attention, intention, and response, evaluated on egocentric streaming video.
