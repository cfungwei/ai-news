### New tool Vernier measures how much resolution time-series forecasting leaderboards have
`research` `models` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

Researchers introduce Vernier, a tool that computes how small a score difference a forecasting leaderboard can actually detect, addressing a gap in how zero-shot time-series models are compared.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Foundation models for time series are pretrained once and then scored on held-out datasets they have never seen, so public leaderboards carry most of the comparative evidence between them. The paper notes that none of these leaderboards currently states how fine a difference in score they can reliably distinguish, a property the authors call resolution. Vernier computes this resolution directly from files a leaderboard already publishes, without requiring new data or reruns. It also reports which specific entries on a board can be meaningfully distinguished from each other at that resolution.

- [Hugging Face Daily Papers: What a Forecasting Leaderboard Can Establish: Resolution Reporting for Zero-Shot Time-Series Models](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.31619)

</details>

### StateWeaver uses multi-agent coordination to keep long AI-generated videos consistent
`research` `models` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

A new method, StateWeaver, coordinates multiple agents to track story-state changes across shots, aiming to fix consistency and event-depiction problems in long AI-generated videos.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Current video foundation models produce high-quality short clips but struggle to preserve consistency over longer videos or correctly depict state changes driven by events in the story. The paper attributes this partly to existing agentic workflows, where continual planning and repair accumulate context while reference updates stay limited. StateWeaver combines what it calls Active States with Hierarchical Collaboration to link review-driven updates to visual references with state inheritance across shots. The authors present it as a way to maintain long-horizon consistency in generated video without the context buildup of prior agentic approaches.

- [Hugging Face Daily Papers: StateWeaver: Multi-Agent Coordination of Story-State Transitions for Long-Horizon Video Generation](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34290)

</details>

### ShowNow speeds up responsive world models with speculative streaming
`research` `models` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

A new framework called ShowNow reduces input latency in interactive world models by using idle computation during playback to pre-generate likely action-conditioned video ahead of user input.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Interactive world models need to generate video continuously while still responding quickly to user actions, and the paper notes that high throughput alone does not solve input latency, since coarse generation can delay action updates and post-input generation adds to the response path. ShowNow is introduced as a fine-grained speculative streaming framework that uses spare computation available during playback to prepare action-conditioned futures before input even arrives. A mechanism the authors call single-latent commitment allows frequent decision points. Details beyond this are not given in the available excerpt.

- [Hugging Face Daily Papers: ShowNow: Fine-Grained Speculative Streaming for Responsive World Models](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34693)

</details>

### DRBench Pro benchmarks deep research agents on knowledge coverage, not just report quality
`agents` `research` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

A new benchmark, DRBench Pro, evaluates deep research agents by how thoroughly their reports cover a topic's knowledge structure, rather than relying only on existing report-quality metrics.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Deep research agents collect information from multiple sources and synthesize it into reports answering complex, open-ended questions. Existing benchmarks judge report quality along several dimensions but, according to the authors, lack a dedicated way to assess which research directions a report actually covers and how deeply each is explored. DRBench Pro makes this structured knowledge coverage its primary evaluation target. Its automated pipeline builds local knowledge trees from Wikipedia to serve as a reference against which an agent's report can be checked for coverage and depth.

- [Hugging Face Daily Papers: DRBench Pro: A Knowledge-Centric Benchmark for Professional Deep Research Agents](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34852)

</details>
