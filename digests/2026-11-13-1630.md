### G-STAR scales token groups to compress autoregressive image generation
`research` `models` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

G-STAR proposes scaling token group size quadratically with spatial compression to address the quality-versus-speed trade-off in discrete autoregressive image generation.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Discrete autoregressive models are a scalable approach to image generation, but they face a trade-off: long token sequences preserve quality while slowing generation, and aggressive spatial compression speeds things up at the cost of representation capacity. Using product quantization, the authors show that under a fixed information budget, token group size should scale quadratically with the per-axis spatial compression factor. Building on that finding, they propose G-STAR, a method based on this group-scaling principle. The excerpt is cut off before further detail, so specifics on results or benchmarks are not available.

### Study asks whether weak decision models can still teach well via RL
`agents` `research` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

Researchers compare three decision models and three general LLMs as actors, judges, and teachers for long-horizon agents, asking if a model that acts poorly can still teach well.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Decision models answer a question with a typed output, such as a probability over a set of options, letting one model choose an agent's actions, judge actions already taken, or score actions to train a policy via reinforcement learning. Prior work has mostly studied these models as actors, judging them by how well they perform the task themselves. This paper instead asks whether a decision model that acts poorly can still teach well as a judge or teacher. The authors compare three decision models against three general-purpose LLMs across these three roles for long-horizon agents. The excerpt ends before stating the paper's findings, so it is not yet clear which role each model type is best suited for.
