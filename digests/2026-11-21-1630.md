Thinking Machines released Inkling 2 Mini, an open looped transformer that reruns some layers per token to reason longer without extra text. It matches Qwen3.8 27B on Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index at about six times lower cost.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Inkling 2 Mini is an open looped transformer released by Thinking Machines. It runs 12 of its 24 layers three times per token, letting it reason internally for longer instead of generating extra text. On Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index, it ties with Qwen3.8 27B while costing roughly six times less to run. The model is described as able to run on a laptop. ### Anthropic claims Claude proved Riemann hypothesis; Stanford publishes rival human proof
`research` `models` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge says Claude produced a Lean-formalized proof of the Riemann hypothesis. Stanford then published a rival all-human proof hours later, and mathematicians are now verifying both.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, says he used Claude to prove the Riemann hypothesis, a problem that has been open for 167 years. The proof is formalized in the Lean theorem-proving language and spans more than 250,000 pages. Just hours after that claim, a separate team of Stanford researchers published their own proof of the same hypothesis, produced entirely by humans. Mathematicians are now independently checking both proofs to see whether either one actually holds up. It is not yet known whether either proof will be confirmed correct.
