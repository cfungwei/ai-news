### New RL method gives turn-level credit to streaming video agents
`research` `agents` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

Researchers propose TurnWise, a turn-level GRPO framework for streaming video models that assigns credit per turn instead of per trajectory, addressing a flaw where shared credit reinforces redundant replies after success and discourages useful follow-ups after failure.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Streaming video models must repeatedly decide whether to respond or keep observing, and existing GRPO-based approaches apply a single trajectory-level advantage across all turns. The paper argues this shared credit scheme is flawed: it can reinforce redundant replies after an earlier success and discourage useful follow-up responses after an earlier failure. TurnWise instead computes credit at the level of individual turns rather than whole trajectories. The excerpt does not give benchmark results or the model sizes tested.

- [Hugging Face Daily Papers: Turns Matter: Advancing Streaming Video RL via Turn-Level Credit](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06728)

### NEO-VLA lets robot action policies read evolving visual computation directly
`research` `robotics` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

NEO-VLA is a vision-language-action model that lets action policies access evolving visual computation directly, rather than relying on a frozen set of features from a separate visual encoder, aiming to preserve signals like geometry and subtle state changes manipulation needs.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Most vision-language-action models compress perception with a visual encoder before action reasoning begins, a fixed endpoint that can discard signals manipulation depends on, such as local geometry, boundaries, depth, and subtle state changes. NEO-VLA instead gives the action policy direct access to the visual encoder's evolving computation rather than a single compressed snapshot. The paper frames this as a question of whether action policies should inherit a fixed visual endpoint or access live visual processing. The excerpt does not specify the model's size, training data, or benchmark results.

- [Hugging Face Daily Papers: From Pixels to Actions - Towards Native Vision-Language-Action Policies](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06918)

### Study: multi-turn RL mainly improves LLM agents' error recovery
`research` `agents` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

A new study finds that multi-turn reinforcement learning's gains for LLM agents depend heavily on how much interaction budget is allowed, and that it mostly improves error recovery and long-horizon behavior rather than uniformly raising single-step decision quality.

<details><summary>More</summary>

The researchers evaluate multi-turn agentic RL using Pass@(K,T) across sampling breadth K and interaction depth T, finding that trajectory-level RL advantages require sufficiently large turn budgets and shrink sharply as those budgets tighten. They also find RL's benefit is not a uniform lift in single-step decision quality; instead, it concentrates in a model's ability to recover from errors over long interactions. ### StreamDuo pairs a live commentator AI with a game-wiki research agent
`research` `agents` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

StreamDuo addresses cross-game streaming commentary, where models trained on one game struggle to transfer game-world knowledge to games they've never seen, by pairing a trainable commentator with a frozen research agent that reads the wiki of the game being played.

<details><summary>More</summary>

The researchers study cross-game commentary and find that current commentators transfer their sense of timing but not their knowledge of the game world to unseen games. StreamDuo combines a trainable commentator model with a frozen research agent that consults the wiki of whatever game is being played, feeding it game-specific knowledge the commentator lacks.
