### Analysis: Thinking Machines' Inkling 2 Small swears in its reasoning traces
`models` `research` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

Vals AI found Thinking Machines' Inkling 2 Small model cursed 316 times across 2,655 benchmark runs, mostly late in failing runs, though 96% of failed runs showed no profanity at all.

<details><summary>More</summary>

The analysis, conducted by Vals AI, examined the internal reasoning text generated by Thinking Machines' Inkling 2 Small model across 2,655 benchmark runs. It counted 316 instances of profanity, concentrated late in long reasoning sequences that ultimately failed. Despite this pattern, the correlation is weak as a predictor of failure: 96 percent of failed runs contained no cursing at all. The findings suggest the swearing is a curiosity in the model's reasoning rather than a reliable warning sign of an impending failed run.

`models` `policy` `research` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

<details><summary>More</summary>

In Vals AI's benchmark of simulated attacks on corporate networks, OpenAI's GPT-6 Sol Daybreak achieved an 80.8 percent success rate on attack objectives. Analysts cited in the coverage say that because open-weight models' capabilities cannot be restricted after release, the practical window for defenders to prepare is narrowing. ### Nous Research strips pro-China bias from Qwen3.8-27B via fine-tuning
`models` `open-source` `research` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

Nous Research retrained Alibaba's Qwen3.8-27B into Arete-27B, cutting pro-Chinese propaganda responses from 22% to under 1% using 53 million tokens of fine-tuning, with capabilities intact.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Nous Research used 53 million tokens of supervised fine-tuning to retrain Alibaba's Qwen3.8-27B into a new model it calls Arete-27B. The rate of pro-Chinese propaganda in test responses dropped from 22 percent to under 1 percent after retraining, while general capabilities reportedly remained intact. The team describes the political bias in the base model as being 'applied late' and 'thin,' sitting on top of the model rather than embedded deeply in it. The result suggests such biases in some open-weight Chinese models can be substantially reduced with comparatively modest fine-tuning effort.

- [The Decoder: State propaganda in some Chinese AI models is only "a thin layer" that fine-tuning can strip away](https://the-decoder.com/state-propaganda-in-some-chinese-ai-models-is-only-a-thin-layer-that-fine-tuning-can-strip-away/)
