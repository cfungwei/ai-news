### Paper: Player-1 teaches game agents to play from synthetic experience
`research` `agents` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

A new paper introduces Player-1, a method for training game-playing agents on synthetic experience instead of large human demonstration datasets, aiming to extend computer-use agents into visually rich games.

<details><summary>More</summary>

The paper argues that while computer-use agents already interpret screens and execute keyboard and mouse actions well, games remain a harder test because of their visual richness and varied mechanics. Existing pipelines for training game agents typically depend on large-scale human demonstrations that cover only a narrow slice of possible game states, and building scalable interactive training environments has been difficult. Player-1 is proposed as a way around this by learning from synthetic experience rather than demonstrations alone. The paper frames this as a step toward more general digital control for AI agents.

- [Hugging Face Daily Papers: Player-1: Learning to Play from Synthetic Experience](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20584)

### Paper: ATAS framework keeps long AI-generated videos spatially consistent
`research` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

Researchers propose Any Time, Any Space (ATAS), a framework that compiles a screenplay into shot requests with explicit location, camera, and timing so long-form AI video generation stays consistent across nonconsecutive scenes.

<details><summary>More</summary>

The paper identifies a gap in current long-form agentic video generation: passing prompts or recent frames between shots doesn't give a model a shared spatial frame to revisit a scene, or a time-indexed record to replay an earlier event. ATAS addresses this by compiling a screenplay into shot requests that carry explicit location, camera, and event-time references. Before generating each shot, the system retrieves relevant context using those references. The approach targets consistency across shots that aren't next to each other in the sequence, a known weak point for video generation models.

- [Hugging Face Daily Papers: Any Time, Any Space: A World-State-Centric Framework for Agentic Video Generation](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20571)

</details>

### Paper: LatentLoop scales looped MoE models with asymmetric compute
`research` `models` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

A new paper studies looped Mixture-of-Experts models that reuse parameters across loop visits, finding the different visits play different roles even though they share weights.

<details><summary>More</summary>

Looped MoE models combine parameter reuse with sparse computation to improve performance under a matched compute budget. The paper's authors learn separate residual coefficients for a model that applies a shared block twice, and observe a smaller scale for the first visit than the second. They test fixing those coefficients and find reducing the first visit's scale further changes model behavior. This supports treating loop visits asymmetrically rather than as identical repeated passes.

- [Hugging Face Daily Papers: LatentLoop: Scaling Looped MoE Models with Asymmetric Compute Allocation](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.19975)

</details>

### Paper: ActionOPD distills agent decisions instead of tokens

<details><summary>More</summary>

ActionOPD instead focuses supervision on decisions rather than individual tokens. The excerpt does not detail how much this improves results over token-level approaches.

### Paper: CriticOPD fixes a flaw in on-policy distillation's teacher supervision
`research` `agents` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

Researchers show that on-policy distillation's assumption, that a teacher can always correct a student's mistakes, fails on wrong rollouts, and propose CriticOPD using error-aware critique to fix it.

<details><summary>More</summary>

On-policy distillation trains a student on its own rollouts with token-level supervision from a teacher, assuming the teacher can reach a correct answer from any prefix the student writes, a property the paper calls recoverability. The authors show this assumption fails on incorrect rollouts: the teacher ends up following the student's errors even when it recognizes them as mistakes. CriticOPD is proposed to recover reliable supervision using error-aware critique instead. The excerpt does not specify which benchmarks or models were used to test it.

- [Hugging Face Daily Papers: Recovering Reliable Supervision via Critique-Guided On-Policy Distillation](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20572)

</details>
