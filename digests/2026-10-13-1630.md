### Multi-Stream Attention Residuals extends depth attention in LLMs
`research` `models` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

A new paper proposes Multi-Stream Attention Residuals, generalizing prior depth-wise attention work to multiple heads for sizable performance gains with little added overhead.

<details><summary>More</summary>

The paper builds on Attention Residuals (AttnRes), a technique for content-dependent retrieval across a network's depth, which acts like single-stream softmax attention along the depth axis. Taking inspiration from how multi-head attention improved sequence-axis attention, the authors generalize AttnRes into multi-head depth attention, which they call Multi-Stream Attention Residuals (MSAR). They report substantial performance gains from MSAR with only minimal added computational overhead. The excerpt cuts off before detailing exactly how stream diversity is drawn from sequence context or which benchmarks the gains were measured on.

- [Hugging Face Daily Papers: Multi-Stream Attention Residuals](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.13630)

</details>

### Study finds retrieved history mostly helps a GUI agent's first step

<details><summary>More</summary>

Personal GUI agents rely on a user's interaction history to interpret ambiguous instructions and anticipate routines. Existing approaches retrieve task-relevant history and feed it into the agent's context, assuming that relevant experience stays useful across every decision within a task. The authors find this assumption doesn't hold: retrieved history strongly helps an episode's first decision, but its benefit is largely spent after that. The excerpt cuts off before describing what the paper proposes as a fix or how it's evaluated.
