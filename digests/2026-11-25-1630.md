### Paper trains AI agents to act on their own stated beliefs, not guess early
`research` `agents` (tagged by fallback) · 1 source

Researchers propose reinforcement learning with verifiable beliefs to stop AI agents from committing to answers before gathering enough evidence, a behavior they call laziness.

<details><summary>More</summary>

The researchers call this behavior laziness: agents commit to an answer before gathering enough evidence, even while their own stated belief still lists several possible answers. Standard outcome rewards only score the final answer, so a lucky guess scores the same as an answer backed by real evidence, giving agents no incentive to keep gathering information. The paper's method, reinforcement learning with verifiable beliefs, instead has a simulator compute the agent's exact posterior during training. This lets the reward reflect whether the agent's belief was actually justified, not just whether its final guess happened to be right. The excerpt cuts off before describing full results, so how much the method improves accuracy isn't detailed here.

- [Hugging Face Daily Papers: Lazy Agents Answer Against Their Own Beliefs: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Beliefs Teaches Them to Act on What They Believe](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.32884)

</details>
